\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
POTLI is a web application for short-term luggage storage at local shops, hotels, and other businesses. It serves travelers who need to leave their bags between transport connections, accommodation check-in times, or other activities. The system supports finding storage on a list or map, reserving a time slot, and verifying drop-off and pickup through a six-digit one-time password (OTP) or QR code. Storage partners manage listings and capacity, record cash collected at pickup, and owe a 15\% platform fee on each completed booking; administrators approve partners, resolve disputes, and record fee settlements.

This report presents the requirements, system design, implementation, and verification of the system. The implementation uses TypeScript and Vite for three role-specific portals (traveler, storage partner, and administrator), Express for the API, Appwrite for authentication and private file storage, and PostgreSQL with Drizzle ORM for application data. Cash on pickup is the only live payment method; online payments, a field pilot, and deployment remain planned. Automated verification comprises 234 backend API tests, 14 frontend unit tests, and 5 end-to-end smoke flows. The primary evaluation metric is Booking Fulfillment Time (BFT), with a pilot target of a median no greater than three minutes; no BFT measurements have been collected yet.

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background and Context}
Travelers may need temporary luggage storage before hotel check-in, after check-out, or while waiting for a transport connection. Carrying bags during these intervals limits movement and access to other activities. Storage facilities at stations and airports may not be near the traveler's destination, and availability may be difficult to establish in advance. Local businesses offer a possible alternative where suitable storage space is available.

POTLI connects travelers with participating businesses and maintains a record of each storage booking. Partners publish their capacity, opening hours, and prices. The system records both luggage handoffs, and administrators review partner details and disputed bookings. Finding a location is therefore only one part of the proposed service; reservation and handoff records are also required.

\section{Problem Statement}
The project addresses the difficulty of finding suitable short-duration luggage storage near a traveler's destination. An informal arrangement may not provide a booking receipt, an availability check, or a consistent record of custody. The system makes these records available without imposing a lengthy procedure on either the traveler or the storage partner.

The interface must be usable on ordinary smartphones and understandable to business owners without technical training. Intermittent connectivity is a further design consideration, particularly during physical handoffs. The project uses standard web technologies and does not require dedicated storage hardware.

\section{Stakeholders}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Traveler:} searches, compares locations, books a slot, presents handoff credentials, tracks status, and rates the experience.
  \item \textbf{Storage partner:} registers a business, publishes capacity and hours, confirms drop-off and pickup, and views earnings.
  \item \textbf{Administrator:} verifies partners, audits bookings and fees, reviews reports, and resolves flagged cases.
  \item \textbf{Payment gateway:} planned external service for UPI collections and refunds; it is not yet integrated, and cash on pickup is used instead.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Deliver a responsive web interface for traveler discovery, account access, partner onboarding, and role-specific operations.
  \item Implement secure role-based authentication and a persistent data model for travelers, storage partners, and administrators.
  \item Build a booking lifecycle with availability validation, QR/OTP-based drop-off and pickup, timestamps, and photographic handoff evidence.
  \item Instrument the workflow so that median BFT and handoff success can be measured objectively.
  \item Record cash collection and the platform fee ledger, provide administrative oversight, and prepare for online payments, repeatable deployment, and later geographic scaling.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Requirements and System Analysis}

\section{Functional Requirements}
\subsection{Traveler Requirements}
A traveler should be able to search by city, landmark, or proximity; compare price, opening hours, and availability; reserve a time window; receive a booking identifier and QR/OTP; verify both physical handoffs; review current and prior bookings; and submit a post-pickup rating.

\subsection{Partner Requirements}
A partner should be able to create a business profile, submit details for verification, define capacity and operating hours, receive reservations, validate handoff credentials, attach a drop-off photograph, restore capacity after pickup, and review earnings.

\subsection{Administrator Requirements}
An administrator should be able to approve or reject partner applications, inspect users and listings, monitor booking states, audit payment and platform-fee records, review performance summaries, and investigate disputes or suspicious listings.

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Usability:} short forms, clear role-specific screens, and a mobile-responsive interface.
  \item \textbf{Security:} credentials held by an identity provider, signed expiring tokens, authorization checks, server-side validation, secret management, and protected handoff evidence. Authentication controls should follow established web guidance~\cite{owasp-auth}.
  \item \textbf{Reliability:} explicit booking states and idempotent state transitions to avoid duplicate confirmations.
  \item \textbf{Performance:} indexed database queries, pagination, compressed assets, and a median BFT target of at most three minutes.
  \item \textbf{Scalability:} stateless API instances and a database structure that can later support indexed location queries.
  \item \textbf{Maintainability:} versioned endpoints, migration scripts, source-controlled diagram definitions, and clear separation of frontend, API, and data layers.
\end{itemize}

\section{High-Level Architecture}
POTLI uses a three-tier web architecture with a shared domain module:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Presentation layer:} a TypeScript application bundled with Vite~\cite{vite} that provides traveler, partner, and administrator portals. Maps use Leaflet with OpenStreetMap tiles, and sign-in uses Appwrite's Web SDK.
  \item \textbf{Application layer:} an Express~\cite{express} API on Node.js under \path{/api/v1}. HTTP middleware provides authentication and role guards, request validation, rate limiting, and upload handling. Routers serve traveler, partner, administrator, file, and system endpoints. A handoff-code service derives OTPs and QR payloads, and a background job expires overdue bookings.
  \item \textbf{Data layer:} PostgreSQL~\cite{postgresql}, accessed through a shared Drizzle ORM~\cite{drizzle} workspace and the \texttt{pg} driver. Appwrite~\cite{appwrite} manages identity, credentials, email verification, and private file buckets for drop-off and listing photos.
  \item \textbf{Shared domain module:} \path{code/shared} holds booking statuses and allowed transitions, pricing and fee calculation, and API types, used by both frontend and backend so that rules are defined once.
\end{enumerate}

The browser signs in to Appwrite and requests a JSON Web Token (JWT)~\cite{jwt} for the current session. It sends the token to the backend using the Bearer scheme. The backend asks Appwrite to identify the account and loads the application profile from PostgreSQL; an account without a profile receives a default traveler profile. Photos are uploaded to the backend, validated, and stored in Appwrite buckets; handoff photos are served only through an authorized backend route.

\chapter{Implementation Details}

\section{Frontend Portals}
The frontend is written in TypeScript, bundled with Vite, and styled with a responsive stylesheet. A hash-based router selects pages for three portals. Travelers search listings by city on a list or Leaflet/OpenStreetMap map, view a price quote, book a slot, see the current OTP and QR code, rate completed bookings, and raise disputes. Partners register through a self sign-up form, manage listings and opening hours, look up bookings, record drop-off with photographs and pickup with cash collected, and view earnings and platform fees owed. Administrators approve or reject partners, moderate listings, cancel bookings, resolve disputes, record fee settlements, review the audit log, and disable users.

Sign-in uses Appwrite's Web SDK to establish a session. The frontend then requests a JWT and calls \path{/api/v1/auth/me} for the profile and role. Login pages check that the returned role matches the selected portal; this controls navigation only, and every operation is authorized again on the server. Cash on pickup is the only live payment method; UPI and card are shown as ``coming soon''. Password recovery and email verification use Appwrite, with mail delivered through Resend. The reset page removes the recovery secret from the URL after use.

\section{Backend API and Middleware}
The backend runs on Node.js and Express with Helmet, CORS, a 100~KB JSON limit, and a global rate limiter. Routers are mounted under \path{/api/v1}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{system and auth:} health checks, \path{POST /auth/login}, and \path{GET /auth/me};
  \item \textbf{traveler:} listings, quotes, bookings, cancellation, ratings, and disputes;
  \item \textbf{partner:} application, profile, listings and covers, handoff lookup, check-in, drop-off, pickup, earnings, and fee settlements;
  \item \textbf{admin:} partner review, users, listings, bookings, disputes, ledger and settlements, and audit; and
  \item \textbf{files:} authorized access to stored photographs.
\end{itemize}
Middleware authenticates the Bearer JWT through Appwrite, loads the profile, and enforces role, verified-email, and approved-partner guards. Request bodies and parameters are validated with schemas. Login is limited to ten attempts per window, per-user limits protect sensitive routes, and uploads are limited to four images of at most 5~MB, checked by file signature as JPEG, PNG, or WebP. A central error handler hides internal details. A background job runs every five minutes and expires bookings that were never dropped off.

\section{Database Schema}
The Drizzle schema defines ten tables: \texttt{profiles}, \texttt{partner\_profiles}, \texttt{listings}, \texttt{bookings}, \texttt{handoff\_records}, \texttt{payments}, \texttt{fee\_settlements}, \texttt{ratings}, \texttt{disputes}, and \texttt{audit\_log}. Profiles link to Appwrite accounts by a unique \texttt{appwrite\_user\_id}; credentials are never stored locally. Partner profiles carry an approval status (pending, approved, rejected, or suspended). Listings store location, capacity, hourly rate, daily cap, opening hours and days, and a status (active, paused, or flagged), with check constraints on ranges. Bookings hold a unique code, bag count, slot, status, quoted and final price in paise, payment method, timestamps for each stage, attempt counters, and an idempotency key. Handoff records are unique per booking and type and store the verifying user, bag count, and photograph file identifiers. Payments record method, status, amount, platform fee, partner share, collection details, and an optional settlement. Enumerations for roles, statuses, methods, and handoff types are database types that mirror the shared module. SQL migrations are applied in filename order, and a seed script creates development accounts through Appwrite's Users API.

\section{Booking Lifecycle and Capacity Transaction}
Allowed status transitions are defined once in the shared module and enforced by atomic updates, so a stale or repeated request cannot move a booking twice. Booking creation runs in one database transaction: the listing row is locked, overlapping bookings that still occupy shelf space are counted (overstaying bags are counted until now), and the booking is inserted only if the requested bags fit. An idempotency key makes a retried request return the original booking, and the traveler is given a unique booking code. The price is quoted from the hourly rate with a daily cap and checked against opening hours.

\section{Handoff Codes and Photographs}
Each handoff stage has a six-digit OTP derived from a server secret, the booking identifier, and the stage (drop-off or pickup) using HMAC-SHA-256; nothing is stored, and comparison is constant-time. The QR payload encodes the booking code, stage, and OTP. Travelers see only the code for the current stage. Five consecutive failed attempts lock the booking's code entry for 15 minutes. At drop-off the partner uploads photographs, which are validated and stored in a private Appwrite Storage bucket; only the file identifiers are stored in the handoff record. Listing photographs use a separate bucket.

\section{Cash Payments and Platform Fee Ledger}
Nothing is charged at booking. At pickup the partner records the cash collected; the final price includes any overstay beyond the booked slot, and the platform fee of 15\% is calculated and recorded on the payment together with the partner's share. Because the partner receives the cash directly, partners owe platform fees to POTLI. Administrators group unsettled completed payments into a fee settlement with a reference, and partners can view their dues and settlement history. Online payment methods remain disabled until a gateway is integrated.

\section{Administration}
Administrators review partner applications (approval is required before a partner can publish listings), flag listings, cancel held bookings, and resolve disputes raised by travelers or partners. Cancelling, approvals, fee settlements, user disabling, and booking expiry write entries to an append-only audit log containing the actor, entity, metadata, and IP address.

\section{Pilot Plan}
A small locality near a railway station or busy market can provide a controlled first deployment. The proposed pilot includes 8--12 partner businesses and 15--20 traveler participants. Before use, partners receive a brief handoff procedure and the team confirms business details. Application timestamps provide quantitative measures, while structured post-use questions capture convenience, trust, and onboarding difficulty. Any safety incident or disputed handoff takes priority over performance measurement.

\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Summary of Work}
POTLI now provides a working luggage-storage service in three portals: search on a list or map, capacity-checked booking, OTP and QR handoffs with drop-off photographs, cash collection with a 15\% platform fee ledger, partner approval, ratings, disputes, an audit log, and administrative settlement. The implementation follows the approved design diagrams, with the cash-on-pickup deviations described in Chapter 3, and the system is covered by 234 backend tests, 14 frontend tests, and 5 end-to-end flows.

Online payments, a field pilot, and deployment remain outstanding. The report therefore makes no performance or BFT claims.

\section{Key Findings}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A booking record retains the information needed to verify custody: handoff credentials, timestamps, and photographic evidence.
  \item Availability and booking creation form the central consistency boundary and are updated in one locked transaction to prevent overbooking.
  \item Identity is managed by Appwrite; PostgreSQL stores roles and business records, and shared transition and pricing rules keep frontend and backend consistent.
  \item With cash on pickup, the platform fee becomes a receivable that needs an explicit ledger and settlement process.
  \item The BFT target is meaningful only once the system is deployed and measured in a pilot.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work and Recommendations}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Integrate a sandbox payment gateway (UPI) and enable \texttt{PendingPayment}, refunds, and automated fee collection.
  \item Deploy a staging environment with managed secrets and monitoring.
  \item Add load tests for nearby search and concurrent booking.
  \item Run the proposed pilot and replace target values with measured BFT and handoff success rates.
  \item Define retention periods for photographs and personal data, and complete a pre-launch security review.
  \item Extend notifications and analytics for partners and administrators.
\end{enumerate}
